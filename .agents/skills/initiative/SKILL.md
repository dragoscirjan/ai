# Initiative

Establish the initiative's starting point, investigate what is missing, and agree on the outcome before writing its brief. A prior research session is optional.

1. Identify the starting point: existing research, a direct user request, or both. Read the referenced material and earlier decisions. If expected research is unavailable, ask for it or explain the missing context. Do not pretend to have read it.
2. With research, extract the relevant findings, evidence limits, and decisions the user already confirmed. Research recommendations are proposals until accepted. Reuse confirmed answers instead of interviewing the user about them again.
3. With a direct request, restate your understanding and inspect the available context. Investigate missing facts within the agreed scope. Ask the user for choices and information only they can provide, not facts you can look up. If a missing fact prevents agreement, name the gap and pause for investigation.
4. Use the required interview to clarify the problem, who it affects, current conditions, reasons for change, desired outcome, success and failure measures, scope, exclusions, constraints, and assumptions. Alternate investigation and questions as needed. Confirm shared understanding before writing. Existing confirmations count; do not repeat settled questions.
5. Once the initiative is defined, resolve where and in what form to save its agreement record. Use a destination and format already specified by the user or applicable `AGENTS.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md`. If either is unclear, ask the user now. Clarify whether to create or update the chosen issue, document, or other record. Do not invent a target.
6. Write and save the agreement record at the authorized destination. Include the agreed points, starting point and context references, acknowledged uncertainties, draft or agreed status with evidence, and the next authorized action. Distinguish evidence, assumptions, and proposals from confirmed decisions. Verify the saved content and report its actual path or URL, format, and agreement status. If a save cannot be verified, say so.

Do not write a speculative brief while material initiative decisions remain unresolved. Report the blockers instead. Technical design and implementation details belong in later specifications and tasks.

Stop after saving and reporting the agreement record. If saving is blocked, provide the prepared content marked "not saved" and explain what is needed. Follow applicable approval and access rules. Agreement on the initiative does not authorize delegation, implementation, or publication beyond the agreed destination.
