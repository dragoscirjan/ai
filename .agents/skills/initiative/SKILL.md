# Initiative

Work out why the effort matters and what outcome the user wants before planning how to build it.

First, check the target project's `AGENTS.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md` for the brief's destination and format. If they leave either unclear, ask the user before drafting. Do not choose chat, a file, or an issue tracker for them.

1. Read the research, project context, and earlier decisions. Establish who has the problem, what happens today, and why a change matters. Do not ask the user for facts the project already provides.
2. Explain your understanding in the user's terms. Use the required interview to settle open decisions without reopening confirmed answers. Ask what success would look like and what would make the effort a failure even if the software worked.
3. Write a short brief covering the problem, desired outcome, success measures, scope, exclusions, constraints, assumptions, and open decisions. Link supporting evidence. Mark your proposals as proposals, not as choices the user made.
4. Record draft or agreed status, the evidence of agreement, and the next authorized action. If the direction is already agreed, record it without starting another interview.

Stop at the brief or unresolved decisions. Technical design, specifications, and execution are separate work. Do not invent them to fill gaps.

Follow project gates. Agreement on the brief does not authorize delegation, implementation, or publication beyond the agreed destination.
