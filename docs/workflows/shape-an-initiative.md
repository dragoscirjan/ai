# Turn an idea into an outcome-level brief

The problem and success measures are less clear than the proposed solution.

**Finish with:** An agreed problem, outcomes, scope, constraints, and success measures.

Arrows show this suggested sequence, not declared `requires` dependencies. Approval arrows are conditional. Declining a gate stops that branch without authorizing its writes. The numbered steps below are the text equivalent of the diagram.

## Before you start

Use a trusted project and follow its instructions. Confirm the requested scope, installed skills, runtime tools, and any required workspace or approval. This selection includes the catalogued instruction requirements [`grilling`](/skills/grilling). Resolve them before use.

## Steps

1. **Describe the problem.** [`initiative`](/skills/initiative) provides the guidance for this step.
2. **Interview about outcomes.** [`grilling`](/skills/grilling) provides the guidance for this step.
3. **Bound scope and measures.** [`initiative`](/skills/initiative) provides the guidance for this step.
4. **Record the agreed brief.** [`initiative`](/skills/initiative) provides the guidance for this step.

## Where to stop

Use research only if facts are missing. A completed brief does not automatically start specification or implementation.

## Optional branches

- Choose [`research`](/skills/research) when a decision depends on facts, alternatives, feasibility, or evidence that you do not yet have.
- Choose [`wait-what`](/skills/wait-what) when the last answer is hard to understand or omits context needed to follow the current proposal.

Optional branches are not prerequisites or permission to launch another phase. You can omit them or stop at the stated result. See [relationship types](/reference/relationships) for the distinction.
