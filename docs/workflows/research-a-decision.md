# Research before choosing an approach

Missing facts could change a technical or product decision.

**Finish with:** An evidence-backed comparison with explicit unknowns and a decision boundary.

Arrows show this suggested sequence, not declared `requires` dependencies. Approval arrows are conditional. Declining a gate stops that branch without authorizing its writes. The numbered steps below are the text equivalent of the diagram.

## Before you start

Use a trusted project and follow its instructions. Confirm the requested scope, installed skills, runtime tools, and any required workspace or approval. This selection includes the catalogued instruction requirements [`grilling`](/skills/grilling). Resolve them before use.

## Steps

1. **Frame the decision.** [`research`](/skills/research) provides the guidance for this step.
2. **Resolve unclear constraints.** [`grilling`](/skills/grilling) provides the guidance for this step.
3. **Gather and compare evidence.** [`research`](/skills/research) provides the guidance for this step.
4. **Report findings and limits.** [`research`](/skills/research) provides the guidance for this step.

## Where to stop

Stop at the research answer. Implementation, dependency installation, and policy changes are different requests.

## Optional branches

- Choose [`wait-what`](/skills/wait-what) when the last answer is hard to understand or omits context needed to follow the current proposal.
- Choose [`initiative`](/skills/initiative) when an idea needs an agreed problem, outcome, scope, constraints, and success measures before solution work.

Optional branches are not prerequisites or permission to launch another phase. You can omit them or stop at the stated result. See [relationship types](/reference/relationships) for the distinction.
